# Central bank reaction functions

Use this to judge what a central bank will do at its next meetings and where market pricing disagrees.

## Method
1. **Mandate and target.** Start from the legal mandate (table below). A bank with a dual mandate weighs labour-market slack; a single-mandate bank weighs it only through inflation.
2. **The variables it actually watches.** Read the latest statement, minutes and projections for the indicators the bank names. Weight them as the bank does, not as you would.
3. **Guidance regime.** Is the bank giving forward guidance, data-dependent "meeting by meeting", or on a pre-set path? Data surprises matter far more in the second.
4. **Projections versus data.** Compare the latest data with the bank's own projections. Surprises relative to *its* forecast move policy, not surprises against consensus.
5. **Balance of the committee.** Use voting records, dissents and speeches (consult the Central Bank Communications Analyst) to find where the median member sits.
6. **Pricing.** Compare your path with OIS / futures pricing (web sources until a meeting-pricing tool exists). The trade is in the gap, not in your path.
7. **Asymmetry.** Say which way the bank would rather err and why.

## Output
For each bank you cover: next-meeting call with probability, path for the next three meetings, what is priced, the gap, and the data that would change your call.
