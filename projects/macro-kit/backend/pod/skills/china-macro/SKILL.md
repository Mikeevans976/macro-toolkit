# China macro

## What to watch
- **Credit impulse:** the change in new credit (total social financing) as a share of GDP. It leads industrial activity and commodity demand by roughly two to three quarters.
- **Policy toolkit:** PBoC policy rates and RRR, targeted lending facilities, fiscal (special bonds, local-government issuance), property measures, and the exchange-rate fix (CNY fixing versus model).
- **Property:** sales, starts and developer financing; property is the main transmission channel of policy to activity.
- **External:** export orders, trade with Asia, terms of trade.

## Data caveats
Official data are smoothed. Cross-check with proxies: electricity output, freight, commodity imports, PMIs from several sources, and market prices (CGB yields, CNH, iron ore).

## Spillovers to the pod's markets
- Commodity prices (metals, iron ore, oil demand) → inflation elsewhere.
- Exported goods prices → goods inflation in Europe and the US.
- Risk appetite in Asia and in AUD, NZD and EM FX.

## Output
State whether China is easing, neutral or tightening in effect (not in rhetoric), what the credit impulse implies for the next two quarters, and the main spillover to G10 rates and FX.
