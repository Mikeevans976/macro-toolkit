# Central bank text analysis

## Statement diffs
- Compare the new statement with the previous one sentence by sentence. List every added, removed and changed phrase.
- Classify each change: guidance, assessment of growth, inflation, labour, risks, balance sheet.
- Score each change from −2 (clearly dovish) to +2 (clearly hawkish); explain each score in one line.

## Speeches and minutes
- Weight by the speaker's vote and influence (chair and core members first; non-voters least).
- Separate the speaker's own view from descriptions of the committee's view.
- Look for repeated phrases across members: coordinated messaging matters more than one speech.

## Hawk-dove lexicon (starting points, not rules)
- Hawkish: "vigilant", "upside risks to inflation", "further tightening may be needed", "not yet confident", "premature to discuss cuts".
- Dovish: "downside risks to growth", "policy is restrictive", "gradual", "can be patient", "data have evolved as expected".
Context changes meaning: "restrictive" is dovish when easing is under way, hawkish when it argues against cuts.

## Output
A net score for the document, the three changes that matter most, and whether market pricing has absorbed them.
