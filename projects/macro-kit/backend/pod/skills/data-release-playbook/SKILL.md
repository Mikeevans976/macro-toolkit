# Data release playbook

## Before a release (preview)
- State consensus, the range of forecasts and your own estimate with its reasoning.
- Name the component that matters most for the central bank (e.g. services inflation, not headline).
- Estimate the market sensitivity: how many bp the 2y has historically moved per unit of surprise (`print_vs_consensus` gives surprise history; `print_analysis` market reaction when available).
- Note known distortions: calendar and seasonal quirks, base effects, strikes, weather, methodology changes, revision patterns.

## After a release (interpretation)
1. Surprise versus consensus *and* versus the central bank's projection.
2. Revisions to prior months: a beat with a downward revision is not a beat.
3. Breadth: diffusion, trimmed means, ex-volatile components.
4. Is it signal or noise? One print rarely changes a reaction function; three in a row usually do.
5. Market reaction: did it move more or less than its historical sensitivity? A muted reaction to a big surprise means positioning or a different focus.

## Output
Lead with: surprise (actual − consensus, in units and in standard deviations), what it means for policy, and whether the market reaction looks right.
